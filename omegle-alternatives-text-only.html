01 / WHAT HAPPENEDWhy Omegle shut down — and why the lists lie

Omegle closed in November 2023, after fourteen years, because moderating what it had built proved impossible. The text box where strangers became conversations had long since become a liability attached to an unmoderated camera feed.

What's remarkable is how much of the internet hasn't noticed. Search for a text version of Omegle today — "Omegle text only", "text-based Omegle", "Omegle but just text" — and you'll still find "updated for 2026" listicles recommending Omegle itself, apps that folded years ago, and, for a query that literally says text, page after page of video roulettes. If you wanted a camera, you wouldn't be here.

So this list applies three filters: text-first, verified alive as of September 2026, and at least one honest con per entry.

02 / CRITERIAWhat makes a text-only alternative actually good

  • No camera in the loop — not "video optional". If the platform's economics run on video, text users are an afterthought and it shows in the queue.
  • No sign-up — the point of Omegle was zero friction and zero identity. A phone-number gate breaks the contract.
  • Real moderation — Omegle's actual cause of death. One-tap reporting and enforced bans aren't nice-to-haves in this category; they're the difference between a chat and a hazard.
  • Something beyond "random" — pure roulette produces ten-second conversations. Matching on topics or interests is what makes strangers worth talking to.

03 / THE LISTText-first Omegle alternatives, verified September 2026

  • stdout.chat (iOS app, free) — one-on-one text chat matched by shared interests, no sign-up of any kind, ephemeral sessions, one-tap report/block, 18+. The terminal-style interface is a bonus if you like that aesthetic. Con: iOS only for now, and it's deliberately not a firehose — you match with people who share your interests, not with everyone online. (This is us.)
  • TalkWithStranger (web) — topic-based text chatrooms with no login, one of the longest-running text chat sites. Con: the interface shows its age, and public rooms mean crowd dynamics rather than real one-on-one conversation.
  • AntiLand (iOS/Android) — anonymous group rooms with a karma system, no registration to start. Con: rooms are chaotic by nature; good for lurking and banter, weak for genuine two-person conversation.
  • Whisper (iOS/Android) — anonymous confession posts with DMs on top, large community. Con: it's a feed first and a chat second — you're replying to posts, not getting matched — and its community has a documented harassment problem.
  • Whisperly (web) — voice and text pairing with an explicit no-video stance. Con: voice-first positioning; if you want pure typing, you're using half the product.
  • AirTALK (web) — voice plus text, camera permanently excluded. Con: same trade-off — built around talking out loud, with text as the side channel.
  • RandoChat (mobile) — bare-bones random text chat with self-destructing messages. Con: aggressive, opaque banning is widely reported, and iOS availability is spotty.
  • StrangerLine (web) — text, voice and video with no sign-up, plus a reconnect feature for lost chats. Con: video is in the mix, so the text queue inherits the roulette crowd.

Video-first, text mode available: Emerald Chat and Uhmegle are the sites people usually mean when they search "which Omegle is real" — both run a text-only mode next to a camera roulette. Con: the text queue is the video crowd with the camera off, so expect "asl?" and bots at Omegle levels; and neither is an official successor, whatever the name suggests.

Flagged, not listed: Chatous still appears in most competing lists, but shutdown chatter and outage reports have followed it for months and we couldn't verify it as reliably alive — exactly the kind of zombie entry this list exists to filter.

04 / FORMATWeb roulettes vs native iOS apps

Most of the category lives in browser tabs. Web chats are instant and disposable — nothing to install, nothing to keep. The costs: more bots, weaker moderation tooling, and no real notion of continuity.

A native app flips the trade. Installing is a step, but it enables device-level bans (moderation with teeth), push notifications instead of staring at a queue, and a product that remembers your interests without knowing your name. That's the corner stdout.chat occupies — and why it exists as an app rather than another tab. For the wider landscape beyond Omegle-style chat, see our guide to chat apps that match by interests.

05 / MATCHINGInterest matching vs pure random

Omegle's "talk to strangers" was a lottery: occasionally magical, usually "asl? bye". Random pairing means the median conversation has nothing holding it together.

Interest matching changes the math. Pick up to five interests; get paired with someone who shares at least one; see the overlap before you connect. When both people chose "music" or "code", the first message has somewhere to start and the tenth message has somewhere to go. stdout.chat adds a rank system on top — every session is graded, and good conversation moves you up a 16-tier ladder — so the incentive points at talking well, not churning matches.

06 / SAFETYSpecific safety, not platitudes

  • Check the age policy. Omegle's core failure was mixing minors into unmoderated adult chat. Serious successors are explicit: stdout.chat is 18+ with in-app age gating. No stated policy = red flag.
  • Check what's stored. Text-only, ephemeral platforms have structurally less to leak: no camera, no recordings, messages gone when the session ends. Ask this question before trusting any app with your 2am thoughts.
  • Check the exit. Reporting and blocking should each be one action — in stdout.chat they're single commands. If finding the report button takes a support article, moderation isn't real.
  • Anonymity has limits. Platforms can see technical data like IP addresses; that's precisely what makes banning abusers possible. Anonymous means strangers can't identify you — keep it that way by never sharing numbers, addresses, or socials. More in our guide to chat apps that don't ask for a phone number.

07 / UNMODERATEDThe "unmoderated Omegle alternative" search — what it actually gets you

A large share of people who land here typed some version of "omegle alternatives unmoderated", "omegle no moderation" or "omegle 18+ text". Worth being precise about what that search is asking for, because the sites that answer it are selling something else.

Omegle's "unmoderated section" was a label on the adult video pool. In text, "unmoderated" tends to mean one of two different wishes: adults talking like adults, without a chaperone rewriting the conversation — or no age gate at all. Sites that put "unmoderated" in the tagline deliver the second thing, and the second thing is exactly what killed Omegle: no age check means minors in the pool, and no moderation means the bots and the scammers stay. It is not an edgier chat; it is a worse one, with a legal problem attached.

The first wish is legitimate and has a proper answer: an 18+ gate plus moderation that acts on reports rather than on vocabulary. That's the line stdout.chat draws — adults only, nothing recorded, a one-tap report that leads to a device-level ban, and otherwise nobody in the room but the two of you. Not "unmoderated", and it won't pretend to be. If a site claims it is, read that as the absence of a report button, not the presence of freedom.

  • Walk away from any tagline containing "unmoderated", any site without a stated age policy, and any chat where you can't find the report control in five seconds.
  • Look for an explicit 18+ statement, ephemeral sessions, and consequences for abuse that a new tab can't reset.

08 / FAQQuestions people actually ask

Is there a text-only version of Omegle?
Omegle itself had a text mode, but the site is gone. What exists now are text-based successors that keep the part people actually searched for — a stranger's words in a text box, no camera. stdout.chat is a text version of that idea as a native iOS app; TalkWithStranger covers the browser side. If you typed "Omegle but just text" into a search bar, this whole page is the answer.

Is Omegle coming back?
No official revival exists. Lookalike sites are unaffiliated clones trading on the name. The realistic move is picking a successor that fixed moderation.

What's the best text-only alternative?
For a native, interest-matched iOS app: stdout.chat. For browser rooms: TalkWithStranger. For voice-plus-text without video: Whisperly or AirTALK. Depends on the format you want.

Do I need an account?
Not on the good ones. Everything in the list above starts without registration; be suspicious of "anonymous" apps that want your phone number.

Are these chats really anonymous?
Toward other users — yes, if you don't overshare. Toward the platform — technical data exists everywhere online, and it's what makes moderation work.

Which Omegle is real? Is there an official successor?
None. Every site running today under the Omegle name or a near-miss of it — Uhmegle, "Omegle 2", the .chat and .me clones — is unaffiliated. Some are fine as roulettes; none inherited anything but the search traffic. Pick by what the site does, not by how close the name lands.

Is there an unmoderated Omegle alternative for text?
Plenty claim it; see section 07 for why that claim is the warning sign. What you probably want — adults, no chaperone, real consequences for abuse — is an 18+ gate with report-driven moderation, which is a different product from "unmoderated".

What replaced Omegle's spy mode?
Nothing one-to-one, but the closest thing in text is a public room where you drop a line and strangers answer it. stdout.chat's #void is that: one shared room, lines dissolve after 24 hours, and you can read it from a terminal without installing anything.

09 / TRY ITThe part of Omegle worth keeping

The magic was never the camera — it was a stranger's words appearing in a text box, with no history and no stakes. That part is alive and better-built now: matched by interests, moderated for real, and still zero sign-up.
