The text-first chat landscape in 2026, sorted by what you actually want from it — real-time conversation, slow letters, crowded rooms, or quiet support. Cons included. Dead apps flagged.

Two disclosures. First: this roundup is by the team behind stdout.chat, and yes, we ranked ourselves first in our own lane — the entry explains why, and every competitor gets an honest read. Second: this list is about meeting new people in text. If you searched "text chat app" meaning a messenger for people you already know, that's a different aisle — see section 04.

01 / SCOPEWhat counts as a text-first chat app

The filter for this list: typed conversation is the product, not a fallback under a video feed; you can meet people you don't already know; and the platform was verifiably alive in August 2026. That last filter matters more than it should — the most-linked lists in this category still recommend apps that shut down years ago.

02 / THE PICKSThe best text-only chat apps by use case (August 2026)

  • Best for real one-on-one conversation: stdout.chat (iOS, free) — interest-matched pairing, no sign-up, ephemeral sessions, and a 16-tier rank ladder that grades every conversation, so talking well is the whole game. Terminal aesthetic included. Con: iOS only, and if you want a firehose of random matches, its deliberate matching will feel slow. (This is us.)
  • Best in a browser: TalkWithStranger (web) — topic chatrooms with no login, running for years. Con: dated interface, and public rooms are ambient noise more than conversation.
  • Best slow correspondence: Slowly (iOS/Android) — pen-pal letters that take hours to "travel" the real distance between you. Genuinely lovely pacing. Con: it's letters, not chat — a reply today is a fast one. Full comparison in our pen pal apps guide.
  • Best crowd: AntiLand (iOS/Android) — anonymous group rooms with a karma system, no registration. Con: rooms are chaotic; one-on-one depth is rare.
  • Biggest community: Whisper (iOS/Android) — anonymous posts with DMs, huge user base. Con: it's a confession feed first; harassment reports have followed it for years.
  • Best voice-adjacent: Wakie / Whisperly — topic-based voice pairing and voice-plus-text with no video. Con: your voice is still exposure; typing it isn't.
  • Best for being heard: 7 Cups — free peer-support chat with trained listeners. Con: it's support, not friendship — the relationship is deliberately one-directional.

03 / IDENTITYWhat each one asks for

The single biggest difference inside this list is what you hand over before the first message:

  • Nothing at all: stdout.chat (no phone, email, or password — random device identity), TalkWithStranger, AntiLand.
  • An account, no phone: Slowly, Whisper, 7 Cups, Wakie.
  • Watch out: plenty of apps marketed as anonymous want a phone number or even a profile photo at sign-up. The full sorting — including the famous names that don't belong on "no phone number" lists — is in our anonymous chat guide.

04 / NOT THIS LISTWhy Signal, Telegram and WhatsApp aren't here

Messengers solve the opposite problem. They connect you with people you already know, and they anchor your identity — usually to a phone number — precisely so those people can find you. Superb tools; wrong aisle. Encrypted protects your messages from third parties. It doesn't make you anonymous, and it doesn't introduce you to anyone.

The apps in this list start where messengers stop: a person you've never met, a conversation with no history, and — on the best of them — no identity to manage at all.

05 / GRAVEYARDApps to stop recommending

  • Omegle — shut down November 2023; still recommended by "updated" listicles in 2026. Anything carrying the name today is an unaffiliated clone. Our text-only alternatives list covers what replaced it.
  • Chatous — long a listicle staple for interest-based text chat; shutdown chatter and outage reports have followed it for months and we couldn't verify it as reliably alive. Flagged, not recommended.
  • Houseparty — gone since 2021, still appearing in roundups. If a list recommends it, the list hasn't been read by its own author in years.

07 / FAQQuestions people actually ask

Best text-only chat app for iPhone?
For meeting new people now: stdout.chat. For letters over weeks: Slowly. Different speeds, both text-first.

Are text chat apps safe?
Structurally safer than video — nothing to record or leak. Real safety is moderation: one-tap reporting, enforced 18+, device bans.

Free, no sign-up?
stdout.chat, TalkWithStranger, AntiLand. Beware "anonymous" apps that want your number — the sorting is here.

Difference from WhatsApp/Signal/Telegram?
Messengers connect knowns and anchor identity; these apps introduce unknowns and (at their best) require none.
