// GUIDES / FIRST LINES

"hey" is where
conversations
go to die.

Ninety openers for texting someone new when all you know is one shared interest — plus the formula behind them, fifteen questions for going deeper, and the art of leaving well.

reading time ~6 min starters 90 + 15 app stdout.chat by the stdout.chat team

Most conversation-starter lists assume you can see a profile: photos to comment on, a bio to reference. But in interest-matched chat — like stdout.chat, where this list comes from — you often know exactly one thing: what you both picked. That's not less to work with. It's better. Here's how to use it.

01 / THE PROBLEMWhy "hey" dies every time

"Hey" isn't rude — it's expensive. It hands the other person the entire job of inventing a topic, proving they're interesting, and carrying you both. Most people, reasonably, decline the job.

A good opener does the opposite: it deposits something first. A reaction, a detail, an easy question — enough that replying is downhill instead of uphill. Everything below is that principle, applied ninety times.

02 / THE FORMULAReact → micro-story → either/or

When you know one shared interest, the reliable shape is: react to the match ("matched on books — good sign"), add a micro-story (one specific detail about you: "I've reread the same novel three times this year"), close with an either/or ("are you a rereader or strictly new stuff?").

Either/or questions are the secret. Open questions ("what music do you like?") feel like forms; either/ors are answerable in five words with zero effort and invite a follow-up naturally. Specific beats clever, easy beats impressive.

03 / THE NINETY90 conversation starters, sorted by shared interest

Music

  • One album on loop for a week or fifty songs on shuffle — which camp?
  • What's the last song you played twice in a row, honestly?
  • Live shows or studio versions? Defend your answer.
  • Which artist would you defend in an argument at 2am?
  • What's in your headphones right now — or what was last?
  • Ever had a song ruined by a person? No names needed.
  • Lyrics person or melody person?
  • What genre would surprise people who think they know your taste?
  • Best concert you've been to — or the one you still regret missing?
  • If your month had a soundtrack, what's track one?

Code & tech

  • Tabs or spaces — and do you actually care, or just enjoy the war?
  • What's the most cursed bug you've ever shipped or met?
  • Dark mode everywhere, or are you one of those daylight people?
  • Which technology do you secretly think is overrated?
  • What are you building right now — work or side project?
  • First language you learned vs the one you'd recommend now?
  • Terminal customized to perfection, or defaults and proud?
  • What's a tool you can't work without anymore?
  • AI: genuinely excited, quietly worried, or tired of the topic?
  • What almost made you quit programming — and what kept you?

Games

  • Comfort game you always return to — what is it?
  • Story campaigns or competitive lobbies?
  • What's the last game that actually kept you up too late?
  • One game you'd erase from memory just to play fresh again?
  • PC, console, or "my phone counts and I will not be shamed"?
  • Hardest boss you've ever beaten — or given up on?
  • Do you finish games or abandon them at 80% like the rest of us?
  • Which game's world would you actually live in?
  • Indie gems or big releases?
  • What are you playing this week?

Films & TV

  • What's your comfort rewatch — the show you've seen three times?
  • One episode a night, or the whole season by 3am?
  • Best thing you've watched this year that nobody's talking about?
  • A film everyone loves that did nothing for you?
  • Subtitles always on, or only for whispery shows?
  • Which ending are you still not over?
  • Horror: yes, no, or only with the lights on?
  • Do you read reviews before or after watching?
  • What's the best opening scene you can think of?
  • If your life were a genre this month, which one?

Books

  • Rereader or strictly new books?
  • What's the last book you couldn't put down — actual last, not the impressive answer?
  • Fiction or nonfiction phase right now?
  • A book you abandoned and feel guilty about?
  • Physical, e-reader, or audiobooks — and do audiobooks count? (They count.)
  • Which book would you hand a stranger with no explanation?
  • Do you finish bad books out of stubbornness?
  • Best opening line you remember?
  • What book hit different because of when you read it?
  • Library person, bookshop person, or "my to-read pile is a structural hazard" person?

Travel

  • Planner with a spreadsheet, or "figure it out at the airport"?
  • Best meal you've ever had while traveling — where and what?
  • Mountains or coastline, forced choice?
  • What place surprised you most — better or worse than expected?
  • Solo travel: freedom or loneliness?
  • What's still at the top of your list, and what's stopping you?
  • Do you learn any of the language first, or point and smile?
  • Cities or nowhere-places?
  • Weirdest thing you've brought home?
  • Where were you at your happiest, geographically?

Fitness & sport

  • Morning workout or evening — and is it choice or survival?
  • What do you actually do: lift, run, climb, something weirder?
  • Do you watch sport, play sport, or both?
  • Team you're loyal to beyond all reason?
  • Best feeling: finishing a workout, or skipping one guilt-free?
  • What got you started — and what keeps you going?
  • Music, podcasts, or silence while training?
  • The one exercise you'd delete from existence?
  • Proudest physical thing you've ever done?
  • Rest days: sacred or suspicious?

Art & design

  • Do you make things or mostly appreciate them? Both counts.
  • What's the last thing — image, building, object — that stopped you mid-scroll or mid-street?
  • Minimalism: calming or boring?
  • Which era's aesthetic should come back?
  • Digital tools or physical materials?
  • A piece of design you think about weirdly often?
  • Museums: slow wanderer or highlights-and-café?
  • What would you make if skill were free?
  • Favorite color that isn't your favorite color's obvious answer?
  • Beautiful-but-useless or ugly-but-perfect?

Late-night philosophy

  • What's a small thing you've completely changed your mind about?
  • Free will: yes, no, or "not before coffee"?
  • What do you think you'll be nostalgic for in ten years that's happening right now?
  • Is a life of interesting problems better than a life of none?
  • What's something everyone seems to want that you genuinely don't?
  • Which question do you wish people asked you more?
  • Does the internet make us more alone or less? One reason.
  • What would you do with a guaranteed decade of anything working out?
  • What's the best advice you've ignored?
  • 2am thought you've never said out loud — this is the venue for it.

04 / GOING DEEPERFifteen questions that earn vulnerability

Don't open with these — arrive at them. After the specifics have gone back and forth for a while, one of these upgrades the conversation. Offer your own answer first; reciprocity is the whole engine.

  • What's something about your interest you've changed your mind on completely?
  • What are you avoiding right now? (Asked kindly.)
  • What's a compliment you received years ago and still think about?
  • What does a perfect ordinary day look like for you — not vacation, ordinary?
  • What's something you're proud of that never comes up?
  • Who took something you said seriously when it mattered?
  • What's the most "you" purchase you've ever made?
  • What skill are you secretly working on?
  • What friendship do you miss?
  • What's a fear you've actually beaten?
  • What do people misread about you on first impression?
  • What's the kindest thing a stranger ever did for you?
  • If nothing could go wrong for one year, what would you attempt?
  • What's a belief you hold that your past self would argue with?
  • What made this week okay — even if barely?

05 / MOMENTUMKeeping it alive: callbacks and open loops

Two habits carry conversations further than any opener. Open loops: end most messages with something liftable — a question, a half-finished thought, a "remind me to tell you about…". Dead ends ("lol true") force the other person to restart the engine every time.

Callbacks: reference details from earlier — the exam they mentioned, the album they defended. A callback says I was actually reading, which in text is the rarest and most felt compliment available.

And answer questions with slightly more than was asked. "Yeah, I run" ends things; "yeah, badly, but there's a bakery at kilometer five" starts them.

06 / EXITSLeaving well is a skill too

Not every match becomes a conversation, and forcing it helps no one. If it's not working after a few honest attempts, leave cleanly: "this was nice — I'm heading off, good luck with the exam" costs five seconds and beats ghosting in every way. On stdout.chat, where sessions are graded and rank reflects how you talk, ending well is literally part of playing well. Everywhere else, it's just decency.

07 / FAQQuestions people actually ask

What do I say instead of "hey"?
React to the shared thing, add one detail of yours, end with an either/or. Pick any line above and it's done for you.

How do I start a deep conversation over text?
You don't start deep — you earn it. Specifics first, then one section-04 question, your own answer included.

How do I keep a conversation going?
Open loops, callbacks, and answers that give slightly more than asked. Section 05 is the whole method.
